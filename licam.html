The full story

LiCam is a portable camera that captures depth instead of color and turns it into stylized 3D images on a phone. I proposed the project and co-lead the hardware, which was the core engineering challenge. I converted a full-size Xbox 360 Kinect into a compact “Kinect Lite”: I stripped it down to its depth/RGB sensor board, removed the tilt motor, fan, audio board, and internal USB hub, and soldered new wiring onto the board’s connector through a custom header. I then built a USB power and data harness with buck converters supplying the board’s lower-voltage rails, replacing the Kinect’s stock 12 V supply so the sensor runs from a Raspberry Pi 4B and battery.

Each capture is a full Kinect depth frame: 640 × 480 pixels with a distance in millimetres stored for each one, about 307,000 depth points. The Pi stores and compresses captures, then sends them over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) in chunks with order tracking and checksum verification. The app reassembles each capture and renders the depth data as an interactive 3D visualization that users can recolor and edit.
